## Integrated chapter: Matter in Our Surroundings

**Source pair:** CBSE 2025–26 Class IX syllabus; user-supplied NCERT *Matter in Our Surroundings*, Chapter 1, 2024–25. The NCERT chapter heading is verified against the supplied PDF.

The official 2025–26 outline names the definition and particulate nature of matter; solid, liquid and gas characteristics; melting, freezing, evaporation/cooling, condensation and sublimation. The NCERT chapter develops inter-particle spaces, motion and attraction, diffusion, temperature and pressure effects, boiling, latent heat, and evaporation factors/cooling. Those detailed concepts are retained as chapter context. Where the NCERT chapter goes beyond the concise syllabus wording—especially pressure and latent heat—the in-app source flags identify it as enrichment rather than claiming extra exam weightage.

The app includes:

- a bilingual lesson/chapter map with source-bounded concepts and learning outcomes;
- three interactive, schematic 3D explorations: particle arrangements across solid/liquid/gas; temperature-linked diffusion; and qualitative surface evaporation, each with a prediction prompt;
- matching existing 2D visual references for states, diffusion and evaporation (the 2D references are existing images, not presented as newly built simulations);
- a temperature-controlled, qualitative pure-water state inquiry around 0 °C and 100 °C at approximately 1 atm;
- source and scope cautions, including that Unit I’s 25 marks are not this chapter’s mark allocation, and that simulation cannot replace practical work.

The temperature inquiry does not model pressure-dependent phase boundaries, latent heat, evaporation rate or molecular trajectories. Near the transition points it labels the phase change without modelling phase coexistence. The supplied CBSE practical 6—determining ice’s melting point and water’s boiling point—remains hands-on work, not a simulated substitute.
